# Open-weight LLMs for university self-hosting (coding assistant + AI-learning) — status as of 2026-09-26

Research method: HF model cards (fetched live), HF Hub API metadata (license tag, safetensors param count, repo creation date — pulled 2026-09-26), vendor blogs, and independent aggregators (Artificial Analysis, SWE-rebench). **Vendor = vendor-reported** unless marked **Indep.** Blank / "—" = not found; nothing was filled in from memory unless marked *(recalled, verify)*.

## 0. Key context before reading the numbers

- **SWE-bench Verified is saturated/contaminated.** Vals AI archived its SWE-bench Verified board on 2026-09-01 ("performance has saturated"); OpenAI deprecated it Feb 2026 over contamination (per morphllm.com summary). Newer model cards (Qwen3.8, GLM-5.2/5.3, DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813, Kimi K3, MiMo V2.6) **stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified** and report SWE-bench Pro, DeepSWE, Terminal-Bench 2.1/3.0/4.0 instead. Numbers across Terminal-Bench **versions are not comparable** (TB 2.0 vs 2.1 vs 3.0/4.0 differ hugely; TB 2.1 scores in the 80s are now common for frontier open models, TB 4.0 in the ~30s).
- **Harness matters.** Qwen3.8 uses the Claude Code harness (temp 1.0, 256K ctx) for SWE-bench Pro/TB; Nemotron uses OpenHands/OpenCode/Codex (Super SWE-V: 60.5 OpenHands vs 53.7 Codex); Kimi uses Terminus-2 for TB 2.0.
- **Independent check (SWE-rebench, fresh tasks, window 2026-05-15 → 07-01)** — much lower than vendor SWE-V numbers, useful for contamination-adjusted ranking: GLM-5.2[high] 62.9%, MiniMax M3 47.2%, MiMo V2.5 Pro 46.5%, DeepSeek-V4 Pro[high] 40.2%, Qwen3.6-27B 31.2%, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 24.7%, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B 17.1%. Source: https://swe-rebench.com/ (Indep.)
- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2** (10 evals incl. Terminal-Bench 4.0, HLE, SciCode, GDPval-AA…), open-weights page fetched 2026-09-26 (Indep.): MiMo-V2.6-Pro 46, GLM-5.3 (max) 45, Qwen3.8 Max 45, Kimi K3 (max) 44, GLM-5.3-Flash 42, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (max) 39, DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (max) 36, Qwen3.8-27B (xhigh) 34, MiniMax-M3 29, Kimi K2.7 Code 26, Nemotron 3 Ultra 23, Gemma 4 31B 19, Gemma 4 26B-A4B 17, Gemma 4 12B 14, Mistral Medium 3.5 14, Nemotron 3 Super 13, gpt-oss-120b (high) 12, Mistral Small 4 11, gpt-oss-20b 9, Nemotron 3 Nano 9, Granite 4.2 3B 9, Llama 4 Maverick 10, OLMo 3.1 32B Think 7. Sources: https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source , https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models . - **Qwen 3.7 was never open-weighted** (Plus/Max API-only); the open line went 3.6 → 3.8.
- **Llama**: no new Meta open LLM since Llama 4 (Apr 2025). **Microsoft Phi**: no new general Phi LLM since Phi-4-reasoning(-plus) (Apr 2025). **OpenAI**: gpt-oss-20b/120b (Aug 2025) still the only open LLMs (+gpt-oss-safeguard). **Seed-OSS**: still only Seed-OSS-36B (Aug 2025).
- **Important additions not in the original list**: Xiaomi **MiMo V2.5 / V2.5-Pro / V2.6 (Flash, Pro)** (MIT, top of AA open index), **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** (180B-A6B, restrictive license), **Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B** (pruned Super), **K-EXAONE 2.0** (Apache-2.0), **Solar Open 2**, **Mistral Medium 3.5 / Small 4**, **Ai2 TMax** (open-recipe terminal agent RL).

## 5. Serving tiers (weights-only estimate + KV headroom; FP8 ≈ 1 B/param, NVFP4/MXFP4 ≈ 0.55 B/param, BF16 = 2 B/param)

NVFP4 requires Blackwell (B200/B300/RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell) for native speed; on Hopper/Ada use FP8 or BF16. Hybrid Mamba/DeltaNet models (Qwen3.5+/3.6/3.8, Nemotron 3) have much smaller KV caches → far higher concurrency per GB than pure-attention models — important for classroom-scale concurrency.

### Tier A — 1× 40–48 GB (L40S, A6000/RTX 6000 Ada, A100-40G)
- **Qwen3.8-27B-FP8** (~28 GB weights; ok on 48 GB w/ moderate ctx; tight on 40 GB)
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (FP8 ~36 GB on 48 GB; very fast, 3B active) / Qwen3.6-27B FP8
- Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B (FP8/NVFP4) / Nemotron 3 Nano FP8 (~32 GB)
- gpt-oss-20b (13 GB MXFP4 — fits even 16–24 GB), Devstral Small 2 24B FP8, Gemma 4 31B (QAT w4a16 ~17 GB, FP8 ~31 GB), Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Granite 4.2 30B FP8, OLMo 3.1 32B (FP8)

### Tier B — 1× 80 GB (H100/A100-80)
- **Qwen3.8-27B BF16** (55 GB) with good KV headroom
- gpt-oss-120b (61 GB MXFP4; runs on H100 via Marlin/triton kernels)
- Qwen3-Coder-Next FP8 (~80 GB — too tight on 80 GB; NVFP4 ~45 GB on RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB)
- Nemotron-Labs Puzzle 75B-A9B FP8 (NVIDIA says fits single H100/H200 "with appropriate batch sizes" — tight)
- Nemotron 3.5 Lightning BF16 (63 GB; NVIDIA states single H100/A100-80)

### Tier C — 1× 141 GB H200 (or 1× B200 192 GB)
- Qwen3.5-122B-A10B FP8 (~125 GB, tight), Nemotron 3 Super FP8 (~124 GB, tight; NVFP4 ~70 GB on B200), Mistral Small 4 119B FP8, Mistral Medium 3.5 128B FP8 (tight), Qwen3-Coder-Next FP8 (~80 GB, comfortable)
- On benchmarks, **Qwen3.8-27B still beats all of these for coding**; use the extra memory for concurrency/1M context.

### Tier D — 2–4× 80 GB (or 2× H200)
- **DeepSeek-V4-Flash** (284B/13B, native FP4+FP8 ≈ ~160–170 GB checkpoint; FP4 experts are Blackwell-native — on Hopper verify vLLM/SGLang FP4-emulation path)
- Qwen3.8-Flash-Next FP8 (~180 GB) — license flag
- MiniMax M2.7 FP8 (~230 GB, 4×80 tight), Solar Open 2 250B FP8, Nemotron 3 Super BF16 (~247 GB), Mistral Medium 3.5 BF16 (~256 GB), Qwen3.5-397B-A17B NVFP4 (~220 GB, Blackwell)

### Tier E — 8× 80 GB / 4× H200 (~560–640 GB)
- **GLM-5.3-Flash** FP8 (~321 GB; MIT; TB 2.1 84.3; AA 42)
- MiMo-V2.5 / V2.6-Flash (311B/15B FP8, MIT)
- MiniMax M3 MXFP8 (~430 GB) — license flag
- Qwen3.5-397B-A17B FP8 (~400 GB)
- Nemotron 3 Ultra: FP8/BF16 needs ≥8×H100 multi-node for BF16; NVFP4 (~310 GB) on 4× B200. NVIDIA: single-node 8× B200 for BF16.
- DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (763B ckpt params, mixed FP4 — likely ~400–450 GB; verify)

### Tier F — B200/B300 class (8× 180–288 GB; 1.4–2.3 TB) — relevant for your B300 lab
- **GLM-5.3** (753B/40B; FP8 ~755 GB; also fits 8× H200) — best AA score among "near-MIT" licenses; GLM-5.2 is the MIT alternative and best independent SWE-rebench open model.
- **DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813** (1.6T/49B, MIT, native FP4+FP8 ~0.9 TB; nvidia NVFP4 + DSpark spec-dec)
- **MiMo-V2.6-Pro** (1.02T/42B, MIT, AA #1 open at 46)
- **Kimi K3** (2.8T/104B, MXFP4 ~1.5 TB → 8× B300; custom license)
- Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B NVFP4 (~1.35 TB → 8× B300; custom license)
- Kimi K2.6 / K2.7-Code (1T INT4 ~600 GB → also fits 8× H200)
- K-EXAONE 2.0 750B (Apache-2.0, FP8 ~750 GB)

## 6. Recommendations

### Best coding model per tier
| Tier | Pick | Why | Runner-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: 1× 40–48 GB | **Qwen3.8-27B-FP8** (Apache-2.0) | TB 2.1 73.0, SWE-Pro 61.7, LCB v6 90.3 at 27B dense; hybrid attn = cheap KV | Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (3B active → high throughput); gpt-oss-20b for ≤24 GB |
| B: 1× 80 GB | **Qwen3.8-27B BF16** | same model, full precision + large KV pool | gpt-oss-120b (Apache, fast, but much weaker on agentic coding: SWE-V 62.4, AA 12); Qwen3-Coder-Next (non-thinking, low latency) on Blackwell NVFP4 |
| C: 1× H200 | **Qwen3.8-27B** (max concurrency) | nothing in the 120B class beats it on reported coding benchmarks | Nemotron 3 Super FP8 (open data, NVIDIA stack, 1M ctx) |
| D: 2–4× 80 GB | **DeepSeek-V4-Flash(-0731)** (MIT) | SWE-V 79.0, TB 2.0 56.9, LCB 91.6, 1M ctx, 13B active | Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (strong, but Qwen Community License restricts "AI Work Assistant" services) |
| E: 8× 80 GB / 4× H200 | **GLM-5.3-Flash** (MIT) | TB 2.1 84.3, DeepSWE 63.4, AA 42; 18B active → good throughput | MiMo-V2.6-Flash (MIT, TB 2.1 87.6 vendor); MiniMax M3 (license flag) |
| F: B200/B300 | **GLM-5.3** or **DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813** / **MiMo-V2.6-Pro** (MIT) | Top AA open scores (45/36/46), TB 2.1 ~88; GLM-5.2 best independent SWE-rebench (62.9) | Kimi K3 (strongest raw, AA 44, but 1.5 TB + custom license) |

### Best fully-open (open data) model for research / post-training
1. **OLMo 3.1 32B Think / Instruct** (Apache-2.0) — the only fully open family: Dolma 3 data, OLMo-core code, all intermediate checkpoints (pre/mid/long-ctx), full post-training pipeline. Weaker in absolute terms (LCB v3 83.3, GPQA 57.5, 65K ctx, AA 7) but ideal for teaching/research on training dynamics. **Olmo-Hybrid-7B** (Gated DeltaNet hybrid, fully open) for architecture research.
2. **NVIDIA Nemotron 3 family (Nano/Lightning 30B-A3B, Super 120B-A12B, Ultra 550B-A55B)** — "open weights + open data + open recipes": pre-/post-training datasets (Nemotron-CC-v2, Nemotron-Post-Training-v3; ~153 datasets / 25T+ tokens), base checkpoints, NeMo RL/Gym recipes; permissive OpenMDW-1.1 (Ultra/Lightning). Much stronger than OLMo; best choice for **post-training / RL research at realistic scale** (Nano/Lightning on 1 GPU; Super on a node).
3. **Ai2 TMax-27B** — open data (TMax-15K) + code for agentic terminal RL on Qwen3.6-27B (weights base not fully open, but recipe is) — good template for student RL projects.

### Other notes
- For AI-learning/general chat on small GPUs: Gemma 4 (Apache-2.0, native function calling, multimodal, 12B/26B-A4B/31B) is the cleanest-licensed US alternative; Granite 4.2 (Apache-2.0, ships official FP8/NVFP4/MXFP4) is enterprise-safe but weaker.
- Avoid for university default: EXAONE 4.5 (NC license), Llama 4 (gated, weak now), Mistral Small 4 is fine license-wise (Apache-2.0 — note one 3rd-party blog wrongly claimed CC-BY-NC; HF tag is apache-2.0).
